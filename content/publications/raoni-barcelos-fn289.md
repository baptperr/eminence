Instagram: 54.6k to 80.4k in four days. That is +47% for a fighter who lost.

Raoni Barcelos headlined his first UFC main event at Fight Night 289, against Raul Rosas Jr. It ended with him unconscious in the middle of the cage after a takedown in round 5. He was stretchered out and taken to hospital. Officially, it is a round-5 loss.

The median loser on that card dropped about 13 skill points. Barcelos dropped 2.9. He had been fighting so well that the fight was named Fight of the Night.

So nobody came for the result. They came for a fight he was fighting well, and for the moment everyone watching feared the worst. That is attention with a reason to care.

Which raises the question this piece is really about: why did a loss like that pay him more than most wins?

Because a crowd that has just watched something frightening wants one thing: to know what happens next. The story was open, and they followed it.

A fighter can't control the night. They can control the next 24 hours. If something happens to you on the biggest night of your career, post the update yourself, the same day: you're awake, here's what happened, here's what's next. Don't leave the hospital story to strangers.
