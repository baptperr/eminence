In 2027, the UFC's star premium will fall below 1.20x. That has never happened. The lowest full year on record is 1.24x, in 2022.

The second call: favourites at 75% or better will be more than 30% of all bouts. That has never happened either. We will check both in January 2028 and publish the result, right or wrong.

## Why now

US pay-per-view has ended. The UFC is paid an average of $1.1B a year for its media rights, and a fight's price no longer depends on how many people will pay to watch it.

The star premium measures that dependence. It is how much more the market values a fight between stars than a comparable fight without them, 1.25x meaning a quarter more. It was already shrinking before the deal. We do not claim the pay-per-view change caused that. We claim the deal removes the one thing that could bring it back.

## Number one: the star premium

From 1 January to 19 September, the like-for-like premium was **1.18x in 2026**, against an average of **1.25x** for the same months in 2021–25.

The honest part: 2025 was 1.07x over those months, and 1.30x for the full year. Late-year star cards can lift the figure by a lot, so a low start proves nothing. That is why this is a call on the full year. Across the years on record, the full-year premium runs from 1.24x to 1.62x. Our call is 2027 below 1.20x, under every year we have measured.

## Number two: the favourites

A heavy favourite is a fighter the market gives a 75% or better chance of winning. Their share of bouts, by year since 2021:

| Year | Bouts with a heavy favourite |
|---|---|
| 2021 | 10.8% |
| 2022 | 16.9% |
| 2023 | 19.4% |
| 2024 | 21.6% |
| 2025 | 24.8% |
| 2026, through 19 September | 28.2% |

*Source: First Light Observatory. 2026 covers bouts with odds available (87% of them).*

It has risen every year and has never reached 30%. Our call is more than 30% in 2027.

## What this means for fighters

If star fights stop earning a premium, the money moves to whoever owns the rest of what a fighter is: the name, the audience, the face. That is the asset First Light invests in, and these are the two numbers we will use to tell whether we are right.

We will publish the 2027 figures in January 2028.
