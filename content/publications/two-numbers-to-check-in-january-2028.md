The UFC used to sell what happened before the fight: the personalities, the stakes, the story. It is now moving to selling what happens inside it, the finish you replay and the beating you cannot look away from. Stars are the expensive part of the first model. Two numbers will show how fast the second one is taking over, and we are calling both for 2027.

**Stars will fight less.** Top-5 fighters have always fought more often than the rest of the roster, between 1.24x and 1.34x as often in every full year since 2021. In 2027 that will fall below 1.20x. It has never happened. In plain terms: a top-5 fighter will fight less than 20% more often than an unranked one.

**The UFC will put on more lopsided matchups.** In more than 30% of 2027 bouts, one fighter will be a heavy favourite, with a 75% or better chance of winning. That has never happened either. In plain terms: nearly one fight in three will be a mismatch on paper.

We will check both in January 2028 and publish the result, right or wrong.

## Why this is happening

The main reason is cost. Stars are very expensive. A promotion that wants to spend less on them has to find entertainment that does not depend on them, and that is what happens in the cage: the crazy finish, the beatdown. A lopsided matchup fits that model, and a star on the card is no longer the point.

Other forces push the same way, and they are part of the story rather than all of it.

- **US pay-per-view has ended.** The UFC is paid an average of $1.1B a year for its media rights, so there is no revenue tied to how a card performs, and no reason to build numbered events and individual fights into must-see stories.
- **The company is corporate now.** The UFC sits inside TKO, a listed company, whose controlling owner Endeavor was taken private by the private-equity firm Silver Lake. Public and private-equity owners are paid for margins and predictability, not for building stars.
- **Enshittification.** Platforms squeeze quality and cost once the audience is captive. A product that no longer has to earn each sale gets cheaper to make.

## Number one: how often stars fight

We compare how often champions and top-5 ranked fighters fight with how often unranked fighters on the roster fight. 1.30x means a top-5 fighter fights 30% more often.

From 1 January to 19 September, the like-for-like figure was **1.18x in 2026**, against an average of **1.25x** for the same months in 2021–25.

The honest part: a low start proves nothing, because late-year star cards can lift a full year by a lot. That is why this is a call on the full year. The full-year range since 2021 is 1.24x to 1.34x. Our call is 2027 below 1.20x, under every year we have measured.

## Number two: the lopsided fights

A heavy favourite is a fighter with a 75% or better chance of winning. The share of bouts that have one, by year since 2021:

| Year | Bouts with a heavy favourite |
|---|---|
| 2021 | 10.8% |
| 2022 | 16.9% |
| 2023 | 19.4% |
| 2024 | 21.6% |
| 2025 | 24.8% |
| 2026, through 19 September (partial) | 28.2% |

It has risen every year and has never reached 30%. Our call is more than 30% in 2027.

![Line chart: share of UFC bouts with a heavy favourite by year, 10.8% in 2021 up to 28.2% in 2026 so far (through 19 September), with the 2027 call marked above 30%.](/publications/two-numbers-to-check-in-january-2028/chart-heavy-favourites.svg)

*Source: First Light Observatory. Fight ranks come from the UFC rankings; win chances come from betting odds, available for 87% of 2026 bouts.*

We will publish the 2027 figures in January 2028.

## What this means for fighters

Stars will be paid less in the cage, and the value of a name will be cashed in outside the sport. If the UFC promotes personalities less, fighters have to promote themselves.

**Nobody is going to build your name for you.**\
**Build it yourself, outside the cage.**
